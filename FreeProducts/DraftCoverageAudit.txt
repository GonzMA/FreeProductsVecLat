DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATION
Definition 2.6 (p. 7): OrderIdeal.IsPrime, VL-Freeproducts.lean:422.
  Improper prime ideals are allowed in Section 2, exactly as the PDF says.
  Section 6 uses properPrimeIdeals, CountableSpectra.lean:33, excluding top.
Definition 6.5 (p. 28): IsCountablePrimeInjective (:146) and
  HasCountablePrimeUniversalDisposition (:155), CountableSpectra.lean.
  The countability condition is on B, not on the target or its Hamel dimension.
  Sublattice-inclusion tests are equivalent to arbitrary embedding tests via
  the range-identification/extension helpers used throughout the proofs.
ClosedCone permits nonconvex cones, and closed polyhedral cones are finite
  unions of convex polyhedral cones. Open polyhedral cones use finite unions
  of nonempty finite lists of strict homogeneous inequalities, as in the PDF.
FiniteArchimedeanClass denotes classes of NONZERO vectors; "finite" excludes
  the extra top class of zero and does not impose finite cardinality.
Free lattices on sets and on real vector spaces are related by choosing a Hamel
  basis. Proposition 2.3 uses IsFreeOver, which directly states the linear
  universal property. Zero-based sequence indices and lifted Boolean indices
  are harmless changes of representation.
Theorem 5.1 uses lattice seminorms; Proposition 5.6 assumes uniform boundedness.
  Both requirements now appear explicitly in the authoritative PDF.
Nonempty compact spaces and the intended nonzero cases are accepted, as requested.

PROOF-STRATEGY VERDICT
The central arguments match: prime-quotient amalgamation; open supports and the
polyhedral distance partner; strong-unit join characters and finite generated
stages; principal-ideal reduction for Banach completion and order density;
finite projections for Banach injectivity; countable cuts and prime-kernel
extension; and the cardinally bounded transfinite pushout construction.
The three previously identified construction differences have been removed:
Example 4.9 now uses the ball/unit-section homeomorphism and sphere projection;
Example 5.4 uses bounded scalar evaluations on the geometric element's zero set;
and the omega-one part of Theorem 6.18 uses transfinite recursion and unions.
Equivalent coordinate formulas, reusable infrastructure and supplied details
remain necessary in Lean; these are described in the individual entries.
This is agreement of proof ideas, not a line-by-line translation.
